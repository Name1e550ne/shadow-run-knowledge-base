Combat in Shadowrun 5th Edition is turn-based and uses Initiative passes to determine action order. Combat can be physical or astral, and involves attack tests, damage resistance, and various tactical modifiers.

Initiative

Initiative determines the order in which characters act during combat. It is calculated at the start of each combat turn.

Initiative Formula

Physical Initiative = Reaction + Intuition + 1d6

Matrix Initiative = Reaction + Logic + 1d6 (for VR)

Astral Initiative = Intuition + Willpower + 1d6

Initiative Passes

Characters may have multiple Initiative passes based on their capabilities:

Source Additional Passes
Base 1 pass
Wired Reflexes (Rating 1-3) +1 to +3 passes
Reflex Booster (Rating 1-3) +1 to +3 passes
Adept Power: Improved Reflexes +1 to +3 passes
Certain Spells/Drugs Varies

Maximum Initiative passes: 4

Example Initiative Roll

Street Samurai with Wired Reflexes 3:
- Reaction: 9
- Intuition: 6
- Base Dice: 1d6 (rolls 4)
- Total: 9 + 6 + 4 = 19 Initiative Score
- Passes: 1 (base) + 3 (wired reflexes) = 4 passes

Combat Turns & Actions

Each combat turn represents approximately 3 seconds of game time. During their Initiative pass, a character can take one Major Action and one Minor Action.

Major Actions

  • Make an attack (melee or ranged)
  • Cast a spell
  • Move full run distance
  • Reload a weapon
  • Ready or stow a weapon
  • Use a complex Matrix action

Minor Actions

  • Move up to run distance
  • Speak a short phrase
  • Drop an item
  • Change firing mode
  • Activate/deactivate cyberware
  • Take cover
Free Actions

Some actions don't count toward your action limit: speaking, dropping prone, recalling knowledge, or observing surroundings.

Attack Tests

Attack tests determine if you hit your target. The basic formula depends on whether you're making a melee or ranged attack.

Attack Type Dice Pool
Melee Attack Strength + Close Combat Skill
Ranged Attack Agility + Firearms Skill
Thrown Weapon Agility + Throwing Weapons
Spell Attack Magic + Sorcery

Hitting Targets

After rolling your attack pool, compare your hits to the target's defense hits (see Defense Tests below).

Damage Types

Shadowrun has three damage types, each affecting targets differently:

Type Color Code Effect
Stun Damage Blue Non-lethal, heals faster, fills Stun track first
Physical Damage Red Lethal, heals slowly, fills Physical track
Overflow Damage Pink Spills over from Stun to Physical when Stun track is full
Damage Tracks

Characters have two damage tracks:
- Stun Track = 8 + Willpower
- Physical Track = 8 + Body
When Physical track fills, the character dies.

Armor & Damage Resistance

When attacked, defenders make a Damage Resistance test to reduce incoming damage.

Damage Resistance Test

Body + Armor Rating = Defense Dice

Each hit reduces the damage by 1.

Damage Calculation

  1. Attacker rolls attack test and gets hits
  2. Attacker calculates base damage (weapon DV + net hits)
  3. Defender rolls Body + Armor
  4. Subtract defense hits from damage
  5. Apply remaining damage to appropriate track

Example

Attack Scenario:
- Attacker hits with 4 successes
- Weapon Damage Value (DV): 8P
- Net hits: 2 (after defender's dodge)
- Base Damage: 8 + 2 = 10P

Defense:
- Defender's Body: 5
- Armor Rating: 6
- Defense Pool: 11 dice
- Defense Hits: 3

Final Damage: 10 - 3 = 7 Physical damage

Combat Modifiers

Various factors can modify your attack dice pool. Apply all relevant modifiers before rolling.

Ranged Attack Modifiers

Condition Modifier
Target behind cover -2 to -6
Target running -1 to -3
Low light conditions -2
Darkness (no vision mods) -6
Range (beyond optimal) -1 to -4
Called shot (body part) -2 to -6
Firing in Full Auto +2 dice pool, increased recoil

Melee Attack Modifiers

Condition Modifier
Charging +1 dice, +1 damage
Flanking +2 dice
Multiple attackers +1 per additional attacker
Off-balance -2 dice

Melee Combat

Melee combat occurs when characters engage in close quarters combat with weapons or unarmed strikes.

Melee Combat Steps

  1. Attacker declares target and makes attack test (Strength + Close Combat)
  2. Defender may make defense test (Reaction + Close Combat or Dodge)
  3. Calculate net hits
  4. Calculate damage (Weapon DV + Strength + net hits)
  5. Defender rolls damage resistance (Body + Armor)
  6. Apply damage

Unarmed Combat

Unarmed attacks use Strength + Unarmed Combat skill. Base damage is equal to Strength.

Ranged Combat

Ranged combat involves firearms, thrown weapons, and projectile weapons.

Range Categories

Range Distance Modifier
Point Blank 0-2m None
Short Range Up to Short range None
Medium Range Short to Medium -2
Long Range Medium to Long -4
Extreme Range Beyond Long -6

Firing Modes

Mode Effect Ammo Cost
Single Shot (SS) Standard attack 1 round
Semi-Auto (SA) +1 dice 1 round
Burst Fire (BF) +2 dice, area effect possible 3 rounds
Full Auto (FA) +4 dice, high recoil 10+ rounds
